# Episode 3, Part 1 — The Cranial-Nerve Map & The Orbit (Blocks 1–2)

**Series:** 3D Skull Anatomy (Remotion + Three.js, real BodyParts3D meshes)
**Status:** Script draft — research-verified, 2026-11-15. No code written; this file is the script only.
**Format (matches Episodes 1–2):** Short segments. Each segment has one English narration paragraph (word-for-word as spoken), an on-screen label in English + Latin, and the Episode 1 "protagonist camera" framing the featured structure. All narration and on-screen text is native professional anatomical English (Terminologia Anatomica terms). No Chinese anywhere in the video.
**Length:** 7 segments, 274 words of narration, ≈ 1 min 31 s at the measured TTS pace (≈ 3.0 words/sec). Per-segment word counts are given so the TTS durations can be measured exactly, as in Episodes 1–2 — measure the real narration durations before locking segment windows.

## Production notes (read before building)

1. **This is the "systems" episode.** Episodes 1–2 taught the parts (bones) and the holes (sutures, foramina). Part 1 teaches the skull as a system: the twelve nerves as one map, and the orbit as one room. The narration keeps pointing back to Episode 2 ("two doorways you already know") so the new knowledge lands on the old.
2. **Block 1 reuses the Episode 2 vault-away staging.** The cranial base, seen from above with the calvaria lifted away, is the only view in which the whole nerve map reads at once. The camera travels across the base front → middle → back, one group per segment, then holds the full map for the recap.
3. **The stylomastoid foramen is the one exception.** It sits on the external (inferior) surface of the base, between the styloid and mastoid processes, so it is invisible from the vault-away top-down view. Segment 3 (cn-back) therefore swings the camera below the skull for its second half — the Episode 2 "below view" staging (segment 13) already exists.
4. **Foramina contents are taught by narration + labels only.** There is no nerve, vessel, or brain mesh. The carotid canal and foramen lacerum are shown by their openings, not as a tube — the mesh is surface-only.
5. **Block 2 needs one new staging idea: sequential wall highlighting.** The orbit's four walls are taught one at a time; each wall's bones stay full-colour while the rest of the skull dims to 45% (the Episode 1 dimming mechanism, applied to a set of bones rather than one bone). The orbit itself is a cavity — its walls are the inner surfaces of the bones, seen by looking into the orbital opening from the front.

## Segment list

### 1. cn-front — The front of the map: CN I–VI
- **Featured:** Cribriform plate, optic canal, superior orbital fissure.
- **Narration (46 words, ~15 s):** "Twelve nerves, ten doorways — here is the whole map. The first nerve threads through the cribriform plate; the second takes the optic canal. The third, fourth, and sixth, with the first division of the fifth, all leave through one cleft — the superior orbital fissure."
- **On-screen labels:** Cribriform plate — *Lamina cribrosa* (CN I); Optic canal — *Canalis opticus* (CN II); Superior orbital fissure — *Fissura orbitalis superior* (CN III, IV, V1, VI).
- **Key facts:** CN I (olfactory) is the only nerve that exits through the anterior fossa, via the cribriform plate. CN II (optic) is the only nerve with its own private canal. CN III (oculomotor), IV (trochlear), VI (abducens), and V1 (ophthalmic) all share the superior orbital fissure — the classic exam grouping, and the reason the fissure, not the canal, is the "eye-movement" doorway. Callback: all three structures were taught in Episode 2 (segments 8–10); this segment is the first time they are named together as one group.
- **Camera:** Vault away. Top-down into the anterior + middle fossae, framing the midline from the ethmoid back to the sphenoid.
- **Staging:** Vault away (Episode 2 staging). All bones full colour; labels point at the three doorways in turn.

### 2. cn-middle — The middle of the map: V2, V3, and the artery's road
- **Featured:** Foramen rotundum, foramen ovale, carotid canal, foramen lacerum.
- **Narration (43 words, ~14 s):** "In the middle, the second division of the fifth nerve leaves through the foramen rotundum; the third, through the foramen ovale. The internal carotid artery has its own road: in through the carotid canal, across the foramen lacerum, and on to the brain."
- **On-screen labels:** Foramen rotundum — *Foramen rotundum* (CN V2); Foramen ovale — *Foramen ovale* (CN V3); Carotid canal — *Canalis caroticus* (internal carotid artery); Foramen lacerum — *Foramen lacerum*.
- **Key facts:** V2 (maxillary) and V3 (mandibular) are the two trigeminal divisions that leave through the greater wing of the sphenoid — the third division, V1, already left through the superior orbital fissure (segment 1), so the whole trigeminal nerve is now mapped. The carotid canal and foramen lacerum are the two foramina Episode 2 deliberately omitted: the internal carotid artery enters through the carotid canal in the petrous temporal bone, then crosses the upper end of the foramen lacerum on its way to the cavernous sinus. The lacerum itself is filled with cartilage in life — nothing passes all the way through it.
- **Camera:** Vault away. Top-down into the middle fossa, framing the greater wing of the sphenoid and the petrous apex.
- **Staging:** Vault away. The carotid canal's internal opening (at the petrous apex) reads from above; its external opening is on the inferior surface and is NOT shown in this segment — the narration says "in through the carotid canal" while the label points at the internal opening (see Accuracy Notes).

### 3. cn-back — The back of the map: CN VII–XII
- **Featured:** Internal acoustic meatus, stylomastoid foramen, jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal.
- **Narration (45 words, ~15 s):** "The seventh and eighth nerves enter the internal acoustic meatus together — but only the eighth stays. The seventh winds through the temporal bone and leaves by the stylomastoid foramen. Nerves nine, ten, and eleven share the jugular foramen; the twelfth takes the hypoglossal canal."
- **On-screen labels:** Internal acoustic meatus — *Meatus acusticus internus* (CN VII, VIII); Stylomastoid foramen — *Foramen stylomastoideum* (CN VII); Jugular foramen — *Foramen jugulare* (CN IX, X, XI); Hypoglossal canal — *Canalis hypoglossalis* (CN XII).
- **Key facts:** CN VII (facial) and CN VIII (vestibulocochlear) enter the internal acoustic meatus together; VIII ends in the inner ear, while VII continues through the facial canal and exits the skull at the stylomastoid foramen, between the styloid and mastoid processes — the nerve's exit is why Bell's palsy (see Clinical Point 1) shows as a one-sided facial droop. CN IX (glossopharyngeal), X (vagus), and XI (spinal accessory) share the jugular foramen with the internal jugular vein. CN XII (hypoglossal) has its own canal, above the occipital condyle. Callback: the internal acoustic meatus, jugular foramen, and hypoglossal canal were taught in Episode 2 (segments 12–13); the stylomastoid foramen is new.
- **Camera:** Two-part move. First half: vault away, top-down/posterior view of the petrous temporal bone (internal acoustic meatus). Second half: camera swings BELOW the skull to the external base, framing the stylomastoid foramen, jugular foramen, and hypoglossal canal from underneath.
- **Staging:** Vault away for the first half; Episode 2 "below view" staging for the second half. The camera move between the two halves is the only new motion in Block 1.

### 4. cn-table — The whole map (recap moment)
- **Featured:** The entire cranial base, all ten doorways.
- **Narration (22 words, ~7 s):** "Twelve nerves, ten doorways. Learn this map and you can read any skull-base scan: find the hole, and you know the nerve."
- **On-screen labels:** All ten doorways at once, as numbered dots with a side legend (the Episode 1 allout treatment): 1 Cribriform plate (I) · 2 Optic canal (II) · 3 Superior orbital fissure (III, IV, V1, VI) · 4 Foramen rotundum (V2) · 5 Foramen ovale (V3) · 6 Internal acoustic meatus (VII, VIII) · 7 Stylomastoid foramen (VII) · 8 Jugular foramen (IX, X, XI) · 9 Hypoglossal canal (XII) · 10 Foramen magnum (XI spinal roots).
- **Key facts:** The ten doorways, in order, front to back: cribriform plate, optic canal, superior orbital fissure, foramen rotundum, foramen ovale, internal acoustic meatus, stylomastoid foramen, jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal, foramen magnum. The carotid canal and foramen lacerum are NOT counted in the ten — they carry the internal carotid artery, not a cranial nerve. The foramen magnum counts because the spinal roots of CN XI pass through it (along with the medulla and the vertebral arteries).
- **Camera:** Vault away. Wide top-down view of the whole cranial base, held steady — the map shot.
- **Staging:** Vault away. Ten numbered dots + side legend (reuse the Episode 1 allout label system). Flag: ten labels at once will crowd — use small numbered dots on the base and the legend at the side, exactly as Episode 1's allout did (see Accuracy Notes).

### 5. orbit-room — One room built from seven bones
- **Featured:** The left orbit (whole).
- **Narration (35 words, ~12 s):** "One room built from seven bones: the orbit. A pyramid with its base open to the face and its apex pointing back into the skull — a socket that holds the eye, and nothing else."
- **On-screen label:** Orbit — *Orbita*. Secondary label: Orbital apex — *Apex orbitae*.
- **Key facts:** The bony orbit is a four-sided pyramid (a cone): its base is the orbital opening on the face, its apex points posteromedially into the skull, and it houses the eyeball and its appendages. Seven bones build it: frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid, lacrimal, zygomatic, maxilla, and palatine — three cranial bones and four facial bones. The orbit is the first functional "room" in the series: Episode 1 taught the bones, Episode 2 taught the doorways, and now the bones are assembled into a space with a job.
- **Camera:** Assembled. FRONT view, close on the left orbit — camera looking straight into the orbital opening, so the cavity reads as a room.
- **Staging:** Assembled. The seven orbit bones stay full-colour; the rest of the skull dims to 45% (Episode 1 dimming, applied to a set of bones). NEW — highlighting a region (seven bones) rather than one or two bones.

### 6. orbit-walls — Four walls
- **Featured:** The four orbital walls, one at a time.
- **Narration (36 words, ~12 s):** "Four walls. The roof: frontal bone and lesser wing of the sphenoid. The floor: maxilla, zygomatic, and palatine. The medial wall: ethmoid, lacrimal, maxilla, and sphenoid. The lateral wall: zygomatic and greater wing of the sphenoid."
- **On-screen labels:** Roof — *Paries superior*; Floor — *Paries inferior*; Medial wall — *Paries medialis*; Lateral wall — *Paries lateralis*. Each wall's label appears as that wall is highlighted.
- **Key facts:** Roof (superior wall): orbital plate of the frontal bone + lesser wing of the sphenoid; it separates the orbit from the anterior cranial fossa. Floor (inferior wall): orbital surface of the maxilla + zygomatic bone + orbital process of the palatine bone; it separates the orbit from the maxillary sinus, and it is the thinnest wall — the one that breaks in a blowout fracture (see Clinical Point 3). Medial wall: orbital plate of the ethmoid + lacrimal bone + frontal process of the maxilla + body of the sphenoid; it is paper-thin (the ethmoid's lamina papyracea) and separates the orbit from the ethmoid air cells. Lateral wall: zygomatic bone + greater wing of the sphenoid; the strongest wall.
- **Camera:** Assembled. Front 3/4 view, holding on the orbit while the highlight moves wall by wall — roof, floor, medial, lateral.
- **Staging:** Assembled. NEW — sequential wall highlighting: for each wall, that wall's bones go full-colour and everything else dims to 45%, then the highlight moves to the next wall. Four beats in one segment; the narration names each wall as its highlight lands.

### 7. orbit-apex — The apex: two doorways you already know
- **Featured:** Orbital apex (optic canal + superior orbital fissure), inferior orbital fissure, supraorbital + infraorbital foramina.
- **Narration (47 words, ~16 s):** "The apex — two doorways you already know. The optic canal carries the second nerve; the superior orbital fissure carries the third, fourth, sixth, and the first division of the fifth. Below them, the inferior orbital fissure carries the second division's zygomatic branch out to the cheek."
- **On-screen labels:** Optic canal — *Canalis opticus* (CN II); Superior orbital fissure — *Fissura orbitalis superior* (CN III, IV, V1, VI); Inferior orbital fissure — *Fissura orbitalis inferior* (zygomatic branch of V2); Supraorbital foramen — *Foramen supraorbitale* (CN V1); Infraorbital foramen — *Foramen infraorbitale* (CN V2).
- **Key facts:** The orbital apex is where the four walls converge, and it has exactly two openings, both in the sphenoid: the optic canal (CN II + ophthalmic artery) and the superior orbital fissure (CN III, IV, V1, VI + superior ophthalmic vein) — both taught in Episode 2, now placed back into the room. The inferior orbital fissure, between the floor and the lateral wall, carries the zygomatic branch of V2, the inferior ophthalmic vein, and sympathetic fibres, and connects the orbit to the pterygopalatine and infratemporal fossae. The supraorbital foramen (V1, supraorbital nerve) and infraorbital foramen (V2, infraorbital nerve) are the two places the trigeminal nerve's first two divisions exit the orbit to the face — forehead and cheek. Clinical anchor: orbital apex syndrome vs superior orbital fissure syndrome (see Clinical Point 2) — the difference is one nerve, CN II.
- **Camera:** Two-part move. First half: vault away, top-down view of the apex from behind (the Episode 2 optic-canal / SOF framing). Second half: camera swings to the FRONT, looking into the orbit and at the face, framing the inferior orbital fissure and the supraorbital / infraorbital foramina.
- **Staging:** Vault away for the first half; assembled front view for the second half. The supraorbital and infraorbital foramina are NOT narrated (they are secondary labels) — they are small, and the narration budget is spent on the apex itself.

| CN | Nerve | Exit foramen / fissure | Latin | Also through the same doorway |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Olfactory | Cribriform plate | *Lamina cribrosa* | — (fila olfactoria only) |
| II | Optic | Optic canal | *Canalis opticus* | Ophthalmic artery |
| III | Oculomotor | Superior orbital fissure | *Fissura orbitalis superior* | — (see below) |
| IV | Trochlear | Superior orbital fissure | *Fissura orbitalis superior* | — |
| V1 | Ophthalmic (trigeminal) | Superior orbital fissure | *Fissura orbitalis superior* | — |
| V2 | Maxillary (trigeminal) | Foramen rotundum | *Foramen rotundum* | — |
| V3 | Mandibular (trigeminal) | Foramen ovale | *Foramen ovale* | Accessory meningeal artery |
| VI | Abducens | Superior orbital fissure | *Fissura orbitalis superior* | — |
| VII | Facial | Internal acoustic meatus → stylomastoid foramen | *Meatus acusticus internus* → *Foramen stylomastoideum* | Labyrinthine artery (in the meatus); stylomastoid artery (at the exit) |
| VIII | Vestibulocochlear | Internal acoustic meatus | *Meatus acusticus internus* | Labyrinthine artery |
| IX | Glossopharyngeal | Jugular foramen | *Foramen jugulare* | — (see below) |
| X | Vagus | Jugular foramen | *Foramen jugulare* | — |
| XI | Spinal accessory | Jugular foramen (+ spinal roots through foramen magnum) | *Foramen jugulare* | — |
| XII | Hypoglossal | Hypoglossal canal | *Canalis hypoglossalis* | Meningeal branch of ascending pharyngeal artery (minor) |
| — | (not a nerve) | Carotid canal → foramen lacerum | *Canalis caroticus* → *Foramen lacerum* | Internal carotid artery + sympathetic plexus; the lacerum is cartilage-filled in life and nothing passes all the way through it |

The superior orbital fissure also transmits the superior ophthalmic vein. The jugular foramen also transmits the internal jugular vein and the inferior petrosal sinus. The foramen magnum also transmits the medulla oblongata and the vertebral arteries — it is counted as the tenth doorway because the spinal roots of CN XI pass through it.

## Orbit — reference table

| Wall | Latin | Bones | Separates the orbit from | Clinical note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Roof (superior) | *Paries superior* | Frontal bone (orbital plate) + lesser wing of the sphenoid | Anterior cranial fossa | — |
| Floor (inferior) | *Paries inferior* | Maxilla (orbital surface) + zygomatic bone + palatine bone (orbital process) | Maxillary sinus | Thinnest wall; breaks in a blowout fracture (Clinical Point 3) |
| Medial wall | *Paries medialis* | Ethmoid bone (orbital plate / lamina papyracea) + lacrimal bone + maxilla (frontal process) + sphenoid (body) | Ethmoid air cells | Paper-thin; infection spreads from the ethmoid sinus into the orbit |
| Lateral wall | *Paries lateralis* | Zygomatic bone + greater wing of the sphenoid | Temporal fossa | Strongest wall |
| Apex | *Apex orbitae* | Sphenoid bone | Middle cranial fossa | Two openings: optic canal (CN II) + superior orbital fissure (CN III, IV, V1, VI) |

Seven bones total: frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid, lacrimal, zygomatic, maxilla, palatine.

## Clinical points (3 — highest yield for Part 1)

### 1. Bell's palsy — the facial nerve at the stylomastoid foramen
**Mechanism (one sentence):** The facial nerve leaves the skull through the narrow stylomastoid foramen, and inflammation or swelling of the nerve there compresses it inside its bony canal, producing a one-sided lower-motor-neuron facial palsy — the whole half of the face droops, including the forehead.
**Supporting detail (not narrated, for key-facts/labels):** Because the lesion is in the nerve itself (lower motor neuron), the forehead is involved — the patient cannot raise the eyebrow or close the eye on that side — which is how Bell's palsy is distinguished from a central (stroke) facial palsy, where the forehead is spared. The nerve's long, narrow bony course (internal acoustic meatus → facial canal → stylomastoid foramen) is why it is so vulnerable to swelling.

### 2. Orbital apex syndrome vs superior orbital fissure syndrome — one nerve apart
**Mechanism (one sentence):** The orbital apex holds two doorways side by side — the optic canal and the superior orbital fissure — so a lesion at the apex knocks out vision (CN II) plus eye movement (CN III, IV, VI) and forehead sensation (V1), while a lesion confined to the fissure spares vision, because the optic nerve takes the canal, not the fissure.
**Supporting detail:** Superior orbital fissure syndrome (Rochon-Duvigneaud): ophthalmoplegia + ptosis + loss of forehead sensation, vision intact. Orbital apex syndrome: all of that plus loss of vision (CN II). The distinction is the classic exam question, and it is exactly the point Episode 2 set up ("CN II is NOT here") — this segment pays it off.

### 3. Orbital blowout fracture — the floor gives way
**Mechanism (one sentence):** A blow to the eye raises the pressure inside the orbit, and the thin floor — the maxilla's orbital surface — fractures downward into the maxillary sinus, trapping the inferior rectus muscle so the eye cannot look up, and producing double vision.
**Supporting detail:** The floor is the thinnest wall and the most commonly fractured; the trapped inferior rectus causes restricted upward gaze and diplopia, and the infraorbital nerve (V2, running in the floor's infraorbital canal) is often injured at the same time, causing numbness of the cheek and upper lip.

## Accuracy notes (flags for the build)

1. **"Ten doorways" count.** The ten are: cribriform plate, optic canal, superior orbital fissure, foramen rotundum, foramen ovale, internal acoustic meatus, stylomastoid foramen, jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal, foramen magnum. The carotid canal and foramen lacerum are NOT counted — they carry the internal carotid artery, not a cranial nerve. State this in the table segment's key facts so the count is never ambiguous.
2. **Stylomastoid foramen visibility.** It sits on the inferior surface of the base, between the styloid and mastoid processes. It is invisible from the vault-away top-down view — segment 3 must swing the camera below the skull for its second half. Flag: the styloid process is small and slender; verify it reads in a test still, and use a marker dot if the foramen itself does not read.
3. **Carotid canal shown by its openings, not as a tube.** The mesh is surface-only: the canal is a tunnel through the petrous temporal bone. Segment 2 labels the internal opening (at the petrous apex, visible from above in the vault-away view). The external opening is on the inferior surface and is not shown — the narration says "in through the carotid canal" while pointing at the internal opening. Do not claim the camera follows the artery through the bone.
4. **Foramen lacerum is not a clean hole.** It is filled with cartilage in life; the internal carotid artery crosses its upper end, and the greater and deep petrosal nerves meet there to form the Vidian nerve. Show it as a labelled region, not as an open hole (same treatment as the cribriform plate in Episode 2).
5. **The orbit is a cavity, not a solid.** Its walls are the inner surfaces of the bones. Looking into the orbital opening from the front, the walls read as surfaces — the "room" framing works. But the apex is deep and small: the optic canal and superior orbital fissure are better shown from the vault-away top-down view (Episode 2 staging) than from the front, which is why segment 7 uses both views.
6. **Supraorbital foramen is often a notch.** In many skulls the supraorbital nerve passes through a notch (*incisura supraorbitalis*), not a complete foramen. The mesh may show a notch rather than a hole — label the region either way, and do not force a hole where there is none.
7. **Inferior orbital fissure is a slit, not a round hole.** It lies between the floor and the lateral wall, connecting the orbit to the pterygopalatine and infratemporal fossae. Show as a labelled region.
8. **TA2 vs TA98 Latin forms.** As in Episode 2: TA2 renamed the sphenoid and ethmoid to *os sphenoideum* and *os ethmoideum*; this script uses the TA98/textbook forms (*os sphenoidale*, *os ethmoidale*). *Foramen stylomastoideum*, *canalis caroticus*, *fissura orbitalis inferior*, *foramen supraorbitale*, and *foramen infraorbitale* are the standard TA forms. *Meatus acusticus internus* (not *porus acusticus internus*, which is only the internal opening) is kept from Episode 2.
9. **Durations are estimates.** Word counts assume ≈ 3.0 words/sec (the measured TTS pace for this series). Measure the real TTS durations for each segment before locking windows, exactly as in Episodes 1–2 (window = narration + 0.6 s).
10. **Feasibility flags, in short:** Block 1 needs no new staging except the camera swing below the skull in segment 3 (the below view already exists from Episode 2). Block 2 needs one new mechanism — sequential wall highlighting (segment 6) — built on the existing dimming system. The recap table (segment 4) reuses the Episode 1 allout numbered-dots + legend system. Nothing in Part 1 requires a mesh that does not exist.
